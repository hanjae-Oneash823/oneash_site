    text: >-

      traveled. Places change, but human warmth doesn't. Given enough time, it's what lets us connect with anyone, anywhere.

  - title: "Epilogue | ça-a-été (That-Has-Been)"
    text: >-
      None of these moments will happen the same way again. The light has moved, the people have gone home, the
      place itself may have changed. A photograph can prove that it happened, and that I was there to experience it.
      Roland Barthes called this ça-a-été, "that-has-been." Twenty moments across four years. Some were planned,
      most were not; some are from far away, others from just down the road. None of them were taken to be shown
      together, and none of them could be taken again. What they share is only that they happened, and that I was there.

I started taking photos because I needed to archive — a defense against how much time and distance had already taken from me. Growing up between Ohio, Texas, and Korea, and now living on the opposite side of the country from my parents, I've rarely had the luxury of staying anywhere long enough to feel entitled to it. So I built a fortress of photographs instead: proof that I had once belonged somewhere, even if I couldn't stay. But somewhere along the way, what my photos were doing for me changed. I stopped needing the images to remember, and started needing them to understand — why a scene that felt completely alien could suddenly feel warm.

That warmth is almost always the work of a person: a grandmother laughing at my bad knife skills, a stranger's silhouette against a skyline, a hand in the corner of a frame. It doesn't take much — we recognize each other as human almost instantly, and that recognition is what turns a landscape into somewhere I could belong. So I'd rather miss a photo than miss the moment, because the point was never really the archive. What started as an anxious need to hold onto things before I lost them has become something closer to a practice: repeated proof that wherever I go, whatever I do, whoever I meet, I'll find that same warmth again.
